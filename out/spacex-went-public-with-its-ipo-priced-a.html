Where things stand

SpaceX priced its IPO at $135 per share for 555.6 million Class A shares on the night of June 11, 2026, raising approximately $75 billion — the largest IPO in U.S. history, more than tripling Saudi Aramco's prior record of $24.9 billion. The $SPCX ticker went live on the Nasdaq Global Select Market and Nasdaq Texas on June 12, 2026. Total investor demand exceeded $250 billion, roughly 3.5–4x oversubscribed, with retail orders alone topping $100 billion. At the IPO price, SpaceX is valued at approximately $1.77 trillion, and Musk retains about 82% of voting power through a dual-class share structure.

The bigger picture

SpaceX's IPO is the largest in U.S. history, more than tripling Saudi Aramco's prior record, and signals a new era of private space infrastructure entering public markets. With Starlink serving 10 million customers across 160 countries and Starship still scaling, quarterly earnings scrutiny will for the first time expose the financial trajectory of the world's dominant launch provider. The deal also raises profound governance questions — Musk's 82% voting control, mandatory arbitration clauses, and Texas corporate law give shareholders unusually limited recourse, setting a precedent that regulators and index-fund investors will be watching closely.
