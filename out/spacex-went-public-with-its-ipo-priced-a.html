SpaceX Blasts Off With Record-Breaking $75 Billion IPO

Elon Musk's rocket-and-AI conglomerate priced 555.6 million shares at $135 each on June 11, surpassing Saudi Aramco's 2019 listing to become the largest IPO in history.

Topic page · generated Jun 12, 2026 5 sources

SpaceX priced its IPO at $135 per share on Thursday, June 11, 2026, raising $75 billion — the largest public offering ever — at a valuation of approximately $1.75 trillion. The company's shares began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker SPCX on Friday, June 12. The offering covered roughly 555.6 million shares, with underwriters granted an option on an additional 83.3 million shares, and up to 30% of the float was reserved for retail investors. SpaceX entered the public markets as a three-division conglomerate spanning launch infrastructure (Falcon/Starship), Starlink satellite connectivity, and the newly acquired xAI artificial-intelligence business.

Why it matters

The SpaceX IPO is the largest in history by a wide margin, instantly making SPCX one of the most valuable publicly listed companies on Earth and forcing passive index funds — including retirement and education accounts — to acquire its shares. The listing also opens SpaceX to mandatory quarterly and annual public financial reporting for the first time, bringing unprecedented scrutiny to its high-stakes bets on reusable rocketry, global satellite internet, and artificial intelligence. With OpenAI and Anthropic expected to follow with their own trillion-dollar-plus IPOs later in 2026, the SpaceX debut is seen as a key test of whether public markets will sustain sky-high AI and space valuations.
