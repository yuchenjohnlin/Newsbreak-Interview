2026 FIFA World Cup Is Underway: Mexico Opens With a 2-0 Win as History Is Made at the Azteca

The largest World Cup in history — 48 teams, 104 matches, three host nations — kicked off June 11 in Mexico City with a drama-filled opener full of goals, red cards, and a star-studded ceremony.

The 2026 FIFA World Cup officially began on June 11 at the iconic Estadio Azteca in Mexico City, with co-host Mexico defeating South Africa 2-0 in a historic, drama-filled opener that saw three straight red cards — a first in any World Cup opening match. Julián Quiñones scored the tournament's first goal in the 9th minute, with Raúl Jiménez adding a second in the 66th. The tournament, jointly hosted by the United States, Mexico, and Canada, is the first to feature 48 teams and 104 matches and will run through July 19. On Day 2, Canada faced Bosnia and Herzegovina in Toronto, and the U.S. Men's National Team played Paraguay in Los Angeles.

Format
48 teams are divided into 12 groups of four; the top two from each group plus the eight best third-place finishers (32 teams total) advance to a new Round of 32, then proceed through single-elimination knockout rounds to the final. Tied knockout matches go to 30 minutes of extra time and then a penalty shootout.

Context and next moves

Reader value beyond the headline
This is the largest FIFA World Cup ever staged — the first with 48 teams, the first co-hosted by three nations, and the first to spread across North America. The expanded format (104 matches, a new Round of 32) gives more nations a path to the title and transforms the tournament into a nearly six-week continental spectacle. For Mexico, the US, and Canada, it represents a massive cultural and economic moment, while for football globally it sets the template for how the sport's biggest event will look for decades to come.
