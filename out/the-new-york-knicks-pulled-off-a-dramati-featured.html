Knicks Complete Largest Comeback in NBA Finals History, Lead Spurs 3-1

OG Anunoby's tip-in with 1.2 seconds left capped a 29-point comeback as New York puts San Antonio on the brink of elimination.

The New York Knicks overcame a 29-point deficit to defeat the San Antonio Spurs 107-106 in Game 4 of the 2026 NBA Finals on June 11, the largest comeback in NBA Finals history. OG Anunoby tipped in a missed Jalen Brunson three-pointer with 1.2 seconds remaining to seal the win at Madison Square Garden. The Knicks now lead the best-of-seven series 3-1 and are one win away from their first championship since 1973. Game 5 is scheduled for Saturday, June 13 in San Antonio.

Reader value beyond the headline
The Knicks are one win away from their first NBA championship since 1973 — which would close the longest title drought in franchise history and the longest gap between championships in NBA history. The Game 4 comeback itself is already cemented as the greatest in Finals history, instantly iconic in New York sports lore. For the young Spurs, led by 22-year-old Victor Wembanyama, squandering a 29-point lead has exposed costly inexperience on the game's biggest stage at the worst possible moment.
  1. Game 5 in San Antonio on Saturday, June 13 (8:30 p.m. ET, ABC/fubo) — Knicks can clinch their first championship since 1973; Spurs must win to stay alive.
  2. Watch for Wembanyama's response: the Spurs star has been the biggest Finals factor when the series has gone San Antonio's way, and he'll need a dominant closeout-game performance to force Game 6.
  3. Monitor NYPD presence around MSG: authorities have pledged increased security after arrests at watch parties for Games 2, 3, and 4; a potential clinching celebration could draw massive crowds.
  4. The Finals MVP race: Anunoby leads the ladder, but Brunson and Wembanyama could leapfrog him depending on who stars in the elimination game.
  5. If the Spurs win Game 5, the series returns to New York for Game 6 on Tuesday, June 16.
